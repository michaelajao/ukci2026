\subsection{Forecasting Accuracy}\label{ssec:e1}

\Cref{tab:forecast_accuracy} reports per-horizon RMSE and mean absolute
scaled error (MASE)
against four baselines under a common
$32$-origin rolling-origin protocol; MASE scales the mean absolute
error (MAE) by the in-sample seasonal-naive($7$) MAE, so values below $1$
beat that benchmark. The \emph{raw} PinnGRU point
forecast (frozen PINN encoder of \Cref{ssec:pinn} with the GRU head of
\Cref{ssec:temporal}) beats all four baselines at $h{=}7$ and $h{=}14$;
a paired Wilcoxon
signed-rank test across the $32$ origins confirms the $h{=}14$ gain
over ARIMA ($-3.88$ MV beds, $p{=}0.003$). It degrades at long horizons, however,
where a regime-shift over-prediction bias (an $h{=}28$ underestimation
rate of only $10.3\%$) leaves it behind ARIMA and seasonal-naive
($+5.53$ MV beds vs ARIMA at $h{=}28$, $p{=}0.006$). The
per-origin recalibration of \Cref{ssec:recal} removes this bias and
blends with persistence at long horizons. The recalibrated model is
best at $h{=}7$, $14$ and $21$, cutting the operational $14$-day RMSE
by $25\%$ over ARIMA and $42\%$ over the per-region GRU, and at
$h{=}28$ ties ARIMA ($28.5$ vs $28.0$) with only seasonal-naive
($26.0$) still ahead. Its $h{=}28$ underestimation rate rises to
$37\%$, so the point forecast is now a well-calibrated median rather
than a systematic over-predictor.

XGBoost and the per-region GRU, lag-only learners bounded by the
$28$-day window, cannot extrapolate into Omicron's altered
case-to-hospitalisation regime and degrade sharply ($h{=}28$ RMSE
$58.9$, $44.0$), whereas ARIMA's per-origin refit degrades only modestly.

Three ablations against the raw PinnGRU ($h{=}14$ RMSE $14.46$, MASE
$0.77$) isolate the load-bearing parts: replacing the pinball loss with a
mean-squared-error (MSE) loss lifts $h{=}14$ RMSE to $18.70$ (MASE $0.97$), skipping PINN
pre-training to $17.27$ (MASE $0.76$), and zeroing the four PINN
parameter features to $27.25$ (MASE $1.23$), the largest regression.
The physics prior, not capacity, drives the gain over the per-region GRU.

\subsection{Allocation Performance Across Solvers}
\label{ssec:e2_e3}

\Cref{tab:allocation} compares the allocation policies on the
seven-region problem with $B = 213$~beds ($20\%$ of total baseline), four-hour
mutual-aid cap, and PinnGRU $q^{(0.1)}\!/q^{(0.5)}\!/q^{(0.9)}$
forecasts at the first Omicron-period origin; every allocation is
re-scored by the common LP re-evaluation over the full three-scenario
set, so $E[u]$ and $u^{\text{worst}}$ are on equal footing.

The \emph{robust LP} achieves the lowest transfer burden
($672$~patient-km, $4.6\times$ below population-proportional and
$1.7\times$ below greedy), but at $\kappa{=}\infty$ this is a tie-break
among policies with identical rounded $E[u]$ and $u^{\text{worst}}$,
not a coverage gain. The \emph{deterministic LP} spends only $51$ of
the $213$ beds and pays $\sim\!3\times$ higher $E[u]$ and
$u^{\text{worst}}$, quantifying the value of $\pi_s$-weighted
expectation over median-only optimisation. The $\kappa{=}10$ column
previews \Cref{ssec:e5_e6}, where a capped transfer service breaks the
tie.

\subsection{Forecast-Quality and Parameter Sensitivity}
\label{ssec:e5_e6}

Substituting each forecaster into the robust LP at the $29$~Dec~$2021$
origin, the $q^{(0.9)}$ peak sums (PinnGRU $1421$, XGBoost $1105$, GRU
$973$, ARIMA $875$, seasonal-naive $745$~beds) all exceed the realised
peak ($797$) except seasonal-naive. Realised unmet is zero throughout,
so forecast quality buys \emph{efficiency} at the $q^{(0.9)}$ margin
rather than coverage in this mild Omicron instance.

Across all $32$ Omicron origins the pattern holds. Robust LP,
population-proportional, and demand-proportional allocations share a
mean expected unmet of $2.9$, but the robust LP cuts mean transfer
burden to $76$~patient-km versus $406$ and $337$. The proportional
baselines fill the full $212.7$-bed budget at every origin, whereas the
robust LP scales surge to local demand (mean $35.4$~beds), so the
routing-efficiency gain holds across the full demand distribution.

Turning to the planning parameters, a one-at-a-time sweep isolates
each control. The budget $B$ dominates
(\Cref{fig:alloc_budget}b). Cutting $B/\sum_r \Gamma_r$ from $0.20$ to
$0.10$ doubles $E[u]$ and triples $u^{\text{worst}}$, while $0.30$
cuts $E[u]$ to $\sim\!1/4$. $\lambda_3$ is \emph{non-binding} at $B=0.20$ (the
expectation-minimising solution already clears the high tail); only a
tighter budget ($\leq\!15\%$) re-engages it.

The transfer controls $\tau$ and $\kappa$ determine when placement
matters. With uncapped recourse on the $12$-link network the
\Cref{tab:allocation} coverage tie persists at $10$--$20\%$ budgets:
coverage depends only on \emph{total} surge, so placement is fungible
and optimisation buys routing alone. Constraining recourse breaks the
tie. Pruning the network to five links ($\tau{=}120$~min) puts the
robust LP ahead of every heuristic on expected and worst-case unmet
($29.9$ vs $32.3$--$34.9$ at $B{=}20\%$), and capping the transfer
service at $\kappa{=}10$ gives the same separation (the $\kappa{=}10$
column of \Cref{tab:allocation}), where greedy matches the LP only by
issuing $213$ oracle calls at $1.4\times$ its transfer burden.
Placement earns its keep when transfers are scarce---the operating
regime of a real surge.
